**The short answer:** a Major is a Counter-Strike event run under Valve's own rules. Valve makes the game. As of November 2026 there are two Majors a year. Each one brings 32 teams through three Swiss stages to an eight-team playoff. Teams don't qualify through open qualifiers: Valve invites them from its own ranking, the Valve Regional Standings.

## How often

As of November 2026, Valve has held two Majors a year:

- In 2025, the BLAST.tv Austin Major, which ended in June, and the StarLadder Budapest Major, from late November to mid-December.
- In 2026, the IEM Cologne Major, which ended in June. Valve's rules name a Singapore Major as the next one.

A different tournament organiser runs each Major. Valve sets the rules that every Major shares, and each organiser adds its own for the rest.

## The stages

Every Major has the same four stages, as of November 2026:

- **Stage 1:** 16 teams play a Swiss stage. Eight of them go through to Stage 2.
- **Stage 2:** those eight teams join eight teams invited straight to Stage 2. They play a Swiss stage, and eight go through.
- **Stage 3:** those eight join eight more invited teams, for a third Swiss stage. Eight go through.
- **Playoffs:** the eight teams left play a single-elimination bracket: quarter-finals, semi-finals and a grand final. A team that loses is out.

### The Swiss stages

In a Swiss stage, teams play opponents with the same record of wins and losses. Three wins send a team through. Three losses knock it out. So every team plays between three and five matches.

A match that can send a team through or knock it out is a best-of-three. Every other Swiss match is a best-of-one. Teams don't meet the same opponent twice in a stage, where that can be avoided.

Who plays whom follows the seeds. Before the Major, all 32 teams are seeded by their place in Valve's world ranking. Within a stage, ties in the record are broken by a difficulty score. It adds up the wins of every opponent a team has played so far, and takes away their losses.

### The playoffs

The quarter-finals and semi-finals are best-of-three. The grand final is a best-of-five: it was one for the first time at the Budapest Major in December 2025, and again at Cologne in June 2026.

Before each series, the teams take turns removing maps from the map pool and picking the ones they want to play. Valve's rules set the order of those turns for every Major.

## How teams get in

- **All 32 places are invitations.** Valve's rules invite 16 teams to Stage 1, eight to Stage 2 and eight to Stage 3.
- **The ranking decides who.** The Valve Regional Standings rank teams by their results at other events through the year: the prize money they earned, the prize money of the teams they beat, how many teams they beat, and head-to-head results.
- **Three regions.** The ranking has three lists: Europe, the Americas and Asia. A team's region is the region most of its players come from.
- **Places per region follow the last Major.** A region gets one Stage 2 place for each of its teams that reached Stage 3 at the previous Major. It gets one Stage 3 place for each of its teams that reached the playoffs.
- **The players are invited, not the club.** The invitation goes to the roster that earned it, and that roster must play. A roster that says no, or no longer has its players, loses its place to the next-highest ranked roster.
- **A last chance.** If no ranked roster is left to invite, a last-chance regional qualifier fills the remaining places.

## Why the Majors matter more

- **One set of rules.** Valve writes the format, the invitations and the seeding, and every Major follows them. Other events each set their own.
- **The game takes part.** During a Major, Valve sells stickers in the game with the teams' logos and the players' signatures. Half of what those sales bring in goes to the organiser, the teams and the players.
- **Viewers play along.** Valve runs a Pick'Em challenge in the game: viewers predict the results of each stage.
- **Souvenirs.** Players can turn an item in the game into a souvenir of a Major match, with that match's team and player stickers on it.

For a viewer, this means the strongest teams of every region in one event, playing to the same rules every time.
