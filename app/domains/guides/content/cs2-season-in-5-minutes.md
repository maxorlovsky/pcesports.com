**The short answer:** the CS2 season runs almost all year. Its high points are the two Majors, events that organisers run under Valve's own rules. A handful of organisers run the other big events: ESL, BLAST, PGL and StarLadder, and a few others. An event counts more when the best-ranked teams play it and it pays more prize money. Valve's ranking of teams counts wins over strong teams, and wins at events with more prize money, for more.

## Who runs the events

Valve makes the game, but it doesn't run most events. Independent companies, the tournament organisers, run them with a license from Valve. The biggest ones, as of November 2026:

- **ESL** runs the ESL Pro Tour. Its two series at the top are Intel Extreme Masters (IEM) and ESL Pro League. ESL calls its two biggest events of the year its Championships.
- **BLAST** runs BLAST Premier. Its events are called Open, Rivals and Bounty. Its schedule for 2027 lists events from January to November.
- **PGL** runs events in Romania, such as PGL Bucharest and PGL Cluj-Napoca, and others in Europe and Asia. PGL runs the Singapore Major in 2026.
- **StarLadder** runs StarSeries. It ran the Budapest Major in 2025. It also runs smaller events for teams below the top level.

The site's match data also covers three more organisers: Perfect World's CS Asia Championships, FISSURE's FISSURE Playground and GAM3RS_X's Thunderpick World Championship. Their matches don't show on this site, because they post no full matches on YouTube.

## Which events count most

### The Majors

The Majors are the biggest events of the year. Valve writes their rules, invites 32 teams in all, from every region, and sells items in the game during each one. [How the CS2 Majors work](/guides/how-cs2-majors-work) explains them.

### Events that invite the best teams

Every other event follows Valve's rules for tournament organisers. As of November 2026, those rules split events into two kinds:

- **A ranked event** counts toward the Valve Regional Standings, Valve's ranking of teams. A team it invites directly comes from that ranking, in order. Other places can go to open qualifiers.
- **An unranked event** doesn't count toward the ranking. It may invite any team it likes, but its total prize money and other pay to teams is capped at 100,000 dollars.

An organiser can also declare a ranked event Tier 1 or Tier 2:

- **Tier 1:** the event invites at least 20 teams from the ranking, from the world list or a region's list. The invitations start with the team ranked first on that list. So the best-ranked teams of that list are offered a place.
- **Tier 2:** if the event invites teams from the ranking at all, it may only invite teams ranked below 12th in the world.

So a Tier 1 event is where the top-ranked teams of its list meet.

### Prize money

As of November 2026, the ranking counts a win over another team for more when it comes at an event with more prize money, up to one million dollars. So wins at big events count for more in the ranking. [How the Valve Regional Standings work](/guides/how-the-valve-regional-standings-work) has the details.

## When the season peaks

As of November 2026, there are two Majors a year. In 2025 and 2026 they fall at the same times of year:

- **The first Major ended in June.** In 2025 it was in Austin, and in 2026 in Cologne.
- **The second Major runs from late November into December.** In 2025 it was in Budapest. In 2026 it is the PGL Singapore Major, scheduled from late November to mid-December.

Between the Majors, the organisers' events follow each other through the year.

The organisers' calendars for 2027, as of November 2026:

- PGL's calendar starts in mid-January, with PGL Bucharest.
- BLAST's calendar starts in January, with BLAST Premier Bounty.
- ESL's calendar starts in late January, with IEM Kraków, whose arena days are in early February. ESL is back in Cologne in August.

## How to follow it without spending all year on it

You don't need to watch every event. A few habits help:

- **Pick the Majors.** Two events a year bring the strongest teams of every region together.
- **Pick the Tier 1 events.** They invite the best-ranked teams of the world list or of a region's list.
- **Watch after the fact.** Several organisers post full matches on YouTube. [Where to watch official CS2 VODs](/guides/where-to-watch-official-cs2-vods) lists them and their channels.
- **Let the site choose.** "What should I watch?" on the [home page](/) picks the finished matches worth your time, without telling you who won.

If you have been away from the game for a while, [CS2 basics for returning viewers](/guides/cs2-basics-for-returning-viewers) covers what changed.
